title: Origin of Long-Period YSO Variations
---

**Background:** Spatially unbiased searches for young stellar objects across the Galactic plane, such as the SPICY catalog, suffer from contamination by asymptotic giant branch stars and long-period variables that share similar infrared colors and excesses.

**Question / Future Work:** Investigate and resolve the physical origin of apparently robust long periods (∼100–500 days) observed in a subset of emission-line young stellar objects that overlap with the characteristic period and amplitude regime of long-period variables. Determine whether these long-timescale variations are driven by disk occultation, binary orbital motion, or other secular phenomena, and establish more robust diagnostic criteria to separate them from evolved pulsating stars.

**Why It Matters:** This question is technically important because it addresses the fundamental ambiguity in time-domain classification where high-amplitude or long-period variability in young stellar objects is misidentified as stellar pulsation, directly impacting the purity of Galactic star formation catalogs.

**Evidence:** For these remaining 10 sources with robust long periods under our stricter criterion, we note that rotational periods of ∼100–500 days are physically unlikely for pre-main-sequence stars... we speculate that they may instead trace longer timescale physical processes (e.g., periodic occultation by disk substructures, orbital motion in an unresolved binary) rather than stellar rotation. These objects illustrate an important limitation of period-based classification: genuine YSOs with long-timescale variability unrelated to stellar rotation can occupy the same period regime as LPVs and may therefore be misclassified by our method.
