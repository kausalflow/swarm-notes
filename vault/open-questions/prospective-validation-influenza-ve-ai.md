title: Prospective Validation of Influenza VE Forecasts
---

**Background:** Preseason forecasting of clinical influenza vaccine effectiveness (VE) relies on quantifying sequence differences between circulating viral hemagglutinin proteins and vaccine target strains, but current mathematical AI models exhibit wide prediction intervals and limited performance gains over historical averages in retrospective evaluations.

**Question / Future Work:** Future work must establish the practical reliability and prospective forecasting utility of sequence-based VE models by conducting rigorous prospective evaluations against final clinical network estimates, expanding independent test datasets, and refining component-specific distance-VE relationships to reduce forecast uncertainty.

**Why It Matters:** Crucial for determining whether mathematical AI models can transition from retrospective analysis to operationally reliable, prospective tools for public health planning and resource allocation.

**Evidence:** Comparison with the corresponding final clinical VE estimates, together with evaluation on additional independent data, is needed to determine whether these sequence-based forecasts can reliably support early-season planning.
