title: Evaluating Recursive QLSTM Robustness
---

**Background:** Hybrid quantum-classical recurrent neural network models, such as Quantum Long Short-Term Memory (QLSTM) and its recursive variants, are typically evaluated under limited simulation conditions, specific single-station datasets, and constrained forecasting horizons.

**Question / Future Work:** Investigate whether the performance gains, convergence stability, and generalization improvements observed with recursive quantum LSTM architectures extend to diverse real-world conditions, including broader geographical stations, longer forecasting horizons, noisy or missing observational data, wider hyperparameter spaces, and execution on actual noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware.

**Why It Matters:** Determining the scalability and robustness of hybrid quantum-classical sequential models across varied real-world operating conditions and hardware backends is essential to substantiate quantum advantage in machine learning.

**Evidence:** Future work should evaluate additional stations, longer forecasting horizons, noisy and missing-data settings, stronger classical baselines, broader hyperparameter ranges, and noisy quantum hardware conditions.
