title: Spatially Dependent Model Identifiability
---

**Background:** Epidemiological compartmental models rely heavily on parameter estimation and identifiability analyses, yet the impact of spatial dependence and multi-scale interactions on practical identifiability remains underexplored.

**Question / Future Work:** Extend practical identifiability frameworks and parameter estimation techniques from standard compartmental ordinary differential equation models to more complex, spatially dependent and multi-scale epidemiological systems.

**Why It Matters:** Spatially dependent epidemiological models introduce significant high-dimensionality and complex parameter correlations that make identifiability analysis vastly more difficult, necessitating dedicated methodological advancements.

**Evidence:** We believe that studies of this kind shed light into key issues in parameter estimation for applications that have real-world importance and are worthwhile to expand upon both in the case of simpler, as well as in that of more complex (and also spatially dependent [32]) models.
