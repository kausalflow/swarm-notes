title: Multi-rotation atmospheric evolution tracking
---

**Background:** Time-resolved spectroscopic monitoring of brown dwarfs and directly imaged exoplanets reveals complex, wavelength-dependent atmospheric variability that can be decomposed using principal component analysis and rotational mapping techniques.

**Question / Future Work:** Investigate the epoch-to-epoch evolution of brown dwarf atmospheres by applying continuous multi-rotation observations in principal-component space to determine whether atmospheric trajectories systematically shift across global states or repeatedly trace stable closed orbits.

**Why It Matters:** Crucial for understanding whether substellar weather phenomena represent stable, long-lived hydrodynamic patterns or turbulent, rapidly reconfiguring meteorological states.

**Evidence:** Continuous, multi-rotation observations would allow this evolution to be tracked directly in principal-component space: whether the atmosphere repeatedly traces a stable, closed trajectory or shifts across the plane to new regions corresponding to different global states.
