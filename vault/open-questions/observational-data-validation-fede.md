title: Observational Data Validation of FEDE
---

**Background:** Time-series forecasting models often evaluate auxiliary predictive power using forecast-error distribution entropy (FEDE) across multiple horizons, yet the performance of this framework on observational data involving incomplete ground truth, feedback loops, and regime shifts remains unvalidated.

**Question / Future Work:** Future work must test whether the synthetic advantages of the FEDE predictive-causality framework—such as functional-form robustness, source specificity, and multi-horizon sensitivity—survive on real observational datasets across domains like finance, climate, epidemiology, neuroscience, and ecology where ground truth is incomplete, feedback loops exist, and competing explanations cannot be controlled by construction.

**Why It Matters:** Crucial for establishing the practical utility and limitations of new machine learning forecasting diagnostics in real-world domains beyond synthetic benchmarks.

**Evidence:** Future work should therefore test whether the synthetic advantages demonstrated here—functional-form robustness, source specificity, and multi-horizon sensitivity—survive on observational datasets where the ground truth is incomplete and competing explanations cannot be controlled by construction.
