title: Multi-class dynamical regime classification
---

**Background:** Topological data analysis and delay-coordinate embeddings can be combined with neural networks to classify nonlinear time-series dynamics, but existing evaluations primarily focus on binary periodic-versus-chaotic regimes.

**Question / Future Work:** Investigate whether the proposed fixed embedding and topological feature set can reliably separate a broader range of dynamical regimes beyond the periodic and chaotic dichotomy, such as quasi-periodic and stochastic dynamics.

**Why It Matters:** Expanding beyond binary classification is crucial for real-world time-series analysis where multiple nuanced dynamical regimes coexist.

**Evidence:** Testing TopTimeNet on a labeled multi-class benchmark spanning periodic, quasi-periodic, chaotic, and stochastic regimes would be a natural way to evaluate these hypotheses.
