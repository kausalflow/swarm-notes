title: Astrometric Constraints on Low-Mass Companions
---

**Background:** Low-mass stellar companions with mass ratios below 0.1 and semimajor axes between 10 and 100 au frequently remain undetected when combining single-epoch high-resolution spectroscopy, high-resolution adaptive optics imaging, and long-baseline radial velocity surveys.

**Question / Future Work:** Investigate alternative observational techniques, such as astrometric accelerations derived from Gaia-Hipparcos data, to constrain and rule out low-mass stellar companions with mass ratios < 0.1 and semimajor axes between 10 and 100 au that survive combined spectroscopic, imaging, and radial velocity vetting pipelines.

**Why It Matters:** Addressing this gap is critical for achieving absolute completeness in stellar multiplicity vetting for high-contrast direct imaging missions like the Habitable Worlds Observatory.

**Evidence:** Surviving stellar companions tend to be low-mass stars with mass ratios < 0.1 and semimajor axes between 10 and 100 au (see Figure 4). We note that it may be possible to rule out these low-mass companions using techniques not explored in this paper, such as by searching for astrometric accelerations using Gaia-Hipparcos data ... but those methods are beyond the scope of this paper.
