title: Global Well-Posedness of Stochastic Transport Models
---

**Background:** Stochastic fluid models such as the rotating shallow water, Great Lake, and compressible fluid equations establish local existence and uniqueness of maximal solutions, but proving whether these solutions can experience finite-time blow-up or exist globally with probability one remains unresolved.

**Question / Future Work:** For complex geophysical fluid models like the stochastic rotating shallow water equations or Great Lake equations, the question of whether the maximal lifetime is almost surely infinite remains open, as global a priori estimates are unavailable and ruling out finite-time blow-up is a major analytical challenge.

**Why It Matters:** Resolving global well-posedness versus finite-time blow-up determines whether stochastic transport models are mathematically sound over long time horizons, which is critical for climate and weather prediction applications.

**Evidence:** In these situations one typically establishes the existence and uniqueness of a maximal solution together with an associated blow-up criterion, while the question of whether the maximal lifetime is almost surely infinite remains open. Developing techniques capable of ruling out finite-time blow-up for these nonlinear stochastic transport equations remains one of the major analytical challenges in stochastic fluid dynamics.
