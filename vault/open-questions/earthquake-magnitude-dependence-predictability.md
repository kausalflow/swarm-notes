title: Quantifying Earthquake Magnitude Predictability
---

**Background:** Earthquake magnitude forecasting traditionally relies on the Gutenberg-Richter marginal law and assumes independence across event magnitudes, leaving the existence and relevance of inter-event magnitude correlations unresolved.

**Question / Future Work:** Determine whether robust, operationally exploitable inter-event magnitude correlations or magnitude-dependent triggering mechanisms exist beyond the Gutenberg-Richter law, resolving conflicting empirical findings from parent-offspring reconstructions, serial dependence tests, and neural conditional models.

**Why It Matters:** Resolving magnitude dependence is critical for advancing earthquake forecasting beyond standard ETAS models and determining the fundamental limits of magnitude predictability.

**Evidence:** Overall, magnitude dependence remains one of the most important unresolved questions in statistical seismology and earthquake predictability.
