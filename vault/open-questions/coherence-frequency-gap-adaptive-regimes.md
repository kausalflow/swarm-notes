title: Coherence-Frequency Gap in Adaptive Regimes
---

**Background:** In adaptive and feedback-driven data regimes, probabilistic forecasts are generated and evaluated under conditions where standard stationary, exchangeable, or i.i.d. assumptions fail, creating a divergence between internal probabilistic coherence and long-run frequency reliability.

**Question / Future Work:** Investigate how to formally characterize and bridge the gap between internal filtration-level probabilistic coherence (under an assessment law) and frequency-level reliability guarantees (under a governing evaluation law) in arbitrary non-stationary and feedback-driven adaptive regimes without relying on strict stationarity or exchangeability.

**Why It Matters:** This is the central foundational question governing the validity of probabilistic predictions in modern AI systems, reinforcement learning, and adaptive experimentation where closed-loop feedback invalidates classical alignment between belief and frequency.

**Evidence:** What assumptions are needed before locally meaningful forecasts support stable frequency interpretations over long interactions?
