title: Adaptive Learning of Donor Transferability
---

**Background:** Donor-based post-shock forecasting methods currently rely on prespecified pre-shore similarity metrics and static aggregation weights rather than dynamically learning transferability and updating relevance as target observations accumulate.

**Question / Future Work:** Further methodological and software development could extend the current donor-based forecasting framework in three key directions: (1) horizon-specific probabilistic response trajectories instead of scalar adjustments applied uniformly across horizons, (2) data-driven learning of donor transferability directly from completed historical episodes rather than relying solely on prespecified pre-shock similarity features, and (3) sequential updating of donor relevance as post-shock target observations become available over time.

**Why It Matters:** Extending the framework to dynamically learn donor transferability and update relevance sequentially addresses a primary limitation of static donor-weighting schemes, enhancing robustness across diverse structural break applications.

**Evidence:** Further methodological and software development could extend the current framework in three direction: horizon-specific probabilistic response trajectories, data-driven learning of donor transferability from completed historical episodes rather than relying only on prespecified pre-shock similarity, and sequential updating of donor relevance as post-shock target observations become available.
