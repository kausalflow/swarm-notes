title: Relaxing Assumptions for Causal Decompositions
---

**Background:** Under treatment interference, standard comparison-group designs identify the difference between the total effect on the treated and the spillover effect on comparison units, rather than separating direct and spillover components.

**Question / Future Work:** Further research is required to evaluate and relax the identifying assumptions—specifically the effect homogeneity across treated and non-treated units and the common support condition in exposure—which are necessary for separating total effects into distinct direct and spillover components when these conditions are implausible in empirical applications.

**Why It Matters:** This determines the methodological boundary between identifying aggregate intervention impacts and recovering structural direct vs. spillover components in observational network settings.

**Evidence:** Where either is implausible, the analysis is confined to the total effect on the treated and the spillover effect on the non-treated, which we regard as the main parameters.
