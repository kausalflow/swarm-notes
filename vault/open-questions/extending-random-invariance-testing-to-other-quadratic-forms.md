title: Extending Random Invariance Testing to Other Quadratic Forms
---

**Background:** Hypothesis testing of second-order properties often relies on quadratic form statistics that possess natural invariance properties under group transformations such as rotations, reflections, or permutations.

**Question / Future Work:** Extending the current concentration-inequality-based random invariance testing framework beyond autocorrelation and time series to other quadratic form statistics—such as Moran's I for spatial correlation, Cramer's test for association in contingency tables, and the Rayleigh test for spherical uniformity in directional data—remains an important direction for future investigation.

**Why It Matters:** Broadening the applicability of analytical concentration-based permutation/rotation tests to other classical quadratic forms allows computation-free exact or beta-corrected p-value calculations across spatial statistics, contingency tables, and directional data.

**Evidence:** Beyond autocorrelation in time series data, our random rotation approach to hypothesis testing can be similarly adapted to other statistical tests in the form of quadratic forms.
