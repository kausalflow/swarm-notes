title: Multi-Model Ensemble Drought Forecasting
---

**Background:** Drought forecasting models trained on historical reanalysis data often struggle to capture lower-tail risk during anomalously dry periods and shifting climate regimes.

**Question / Future Work:** Future research should explore extending climate-variability-aware forecasting frameworks to multi-model ensembles to reduce sensitivity to individual driving-model biases, evaluate non-Gaussian tail models for compound-event seasons, incorporate additional land-surface and snow-process predictors to resolve local failure modes in complex terrain, and test integration with operational seasonal prediction systems.

**Why It Matters:** Crucial for scaling probabilistic deep learning frameworks across diverse climate models and reducing regional model biases in extreme weather forecasting.

**Evidence:** Future work should extend the approach to multi-model ensembles to reduce sensitivity to driving-model biases, evaluate non-Gaussian tail models for compound-event seasons, include additional land-surface and snow-process predictors to address the Alpine and British Isles failure modes, and test integration with operational seasonal prediction systems.
