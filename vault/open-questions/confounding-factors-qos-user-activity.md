title: Confounders in QoS-Activity Relationships
---

**Background:** Remote Desktop Services (RDS) rely heavily on network Quality of Service (QoS), yet real-world observational studies examining the fine-grained temporal dynamics of QoS and user behavior remain rare compared to controlled laboratory experiments.

**Question / Future Work:** Investigate potential confounding factors such as application types, user contexts, and time-of-day effects that may simultaneously influence both QoS metrics and user activity in real-world remote desktop service deployments.

**Why It Matters:** Essential for establishing causal relationships rather than mere correlations between network unpredictability metrics and user engagement in distributed enterprise systems.

**Evidence:** (a) Confounding factors. A common factor may influence both QoS and user activity (and hence the packet/byte counts). Candidates include application type, user context, and time-of-day effects... We have not yet identified specific confounders of this kind; investigating them is left for future work.
