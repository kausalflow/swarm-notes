title: Optimizing Deep Observation Assimilation Strategies
---

**Background:** Regional ocean forecasting models rely heavily on data assimilation methods that primarily constrain the upper ocean (above 1000 m depth) while leaving the deep ocean unconstrained, despite deep circulation playing a critical role in developing full water-column dynamics and boundary current variability.

**Question / Future Work:** Determine the specific spatial and temporal conditions under which deep ocean observations are most critical for regional ensemble forecast success, and evaluate the specific impacts of assimilating deep ocean data (such as bottom pressure and near-bottom currents) via Observing System Experiments (OSEs) and Observing System Simulation Experiments (OSSEs).

**Why It Matters:** Understanding the spatial and temporal sensitivity of deep ocean observations is crucial for designing cost-effective observational networks and data assimilation systems that can accurately constrain both upper and deep ocean circulation.

**Evidence:** In addition, these results indicate that deep circulation matters, but our understanding of when and where these observations are most critical for forecast success is not well known. Another study could follow Dukhovskoy et al. (2023) and conduct an Observing System Experiment (OSE) and an Observing System Simulation Experiment (OSSE).
