title: Mitigation of Terminal Noise in SOP Sensing
---

**Background:** State-of-polarization (SOP) sensing via coherent transponders in submarine optical fiber cables is fundamentally limited by a combination of fiber-induced polarization drift and extrinsic noise originating from terminal hardware and amplifiers.

**Question / Future Work:** Identify, isolate, and mitigate the specific contributions of terminal hardware noise, amplification noise, and transponder-related polarization artifacts from environmental fiber-optic signals to maximize the sensitivity and detection limits of wide-area geophysical and seismic monitoring systems.

**Why It Matters:** Terminal and hardware noise acts as a primary bottleneck preventing coherent transponder-based polarization sensing from achieving its theoretical sensitivity limit, making noise mitigation critical for reliable planetary-scale geophysical monitoring.

**Evidence:** The results highlight the need to identify and mitigate terminal noise sources to improve sensing sensitivity and further enable the use of existing submarine telecommunication cables for large-scale environmental and geophysical monitoring.
