title: Prospective Validation of Dual-Parameter Forecasting
---

**Background:** Spatially resolved stress-sensitive predictors (such as the b-value) and rate-based seismicity models are increasingly combined for earthquake forecasting, yet establishing their non-redundant information gain remains methodologically challenging across different tectonic regions and sample sizes.

**Question / Future Work:** Future research needs to determine whether the apparent incremental predictive capability of stress-sensitive b-values over rate-based background seismicity models holds true across larger independent target datasets, different magnitude thresholds, and diverse tectonic settings beyond regional collision zones like the Zagros.

**Why It Matters:** Resolving whether stress-based parameters genuinely improve forecasting skill over rate-based baselines is central to advancing physics-based operational earthquake forecasting models.

**Evidence:** For Mw ≥ 5.5 (N = 15), however, the calibrated b-threshold (qb∗ ≈ 0.97) is effectively inactive, and the model reduces to μ alone; whether this reflects the small sample or a genuine magnitude dependence requires prospective validation.
