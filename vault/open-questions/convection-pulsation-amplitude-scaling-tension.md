title: Convection-Pulsation Amplitude Scaling Tension
---

**Background:** State-of-the-art 3D surface convection simulations predict a mode-amplitude scaling relation with stellar parameters that deviates noticeably from empirical observations in cool, late-type dwarfs.

**Question / Future Work:** Further investigation is needed to reconcile the tension between the empirical mode-amplitude scaling (such as the steep (L/M)^{1.5} dependence observed for K dwarfs) and the flatter scaling predicted by first-principles 3D surface convection simulations (approximately (L/M)^{0.9}), which points to missing physical ingredients such as magnetic fields, rotation, or line-formation effects in convection-pulsation coupling models.

**Why It Matters:** Resolving this discrepancy is crucial for understanding the dynamical coupling between turbulence, convection, and pulsations in cool stars, as well as accurately predicting seismic yields for future space missions like PLATO and Habitable Worlds Observatory.

**Evidence:** Strikingly, the 3D convection simulations are unable to recover the steep (s\approx 1.5) empirical mode-amplitude scaling of K dwarfs. The mismatch remains robust when the uncertainties in the derived velocities are taken into account... This result therefore highlights a clear tension between first-principles convection modeling and the observed amplitude behavior of solar-like oscillations in cool dwarfs.
