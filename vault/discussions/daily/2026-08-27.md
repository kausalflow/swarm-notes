### Architectural Trends, Analytical Frameworks, and Domain Applications in Modern Time-Series & Signal Analysis

The latest research cohort reflects a maturation across multiple fronts: a pivot toward rigorous cost-performance tradeoffs in foundation models, the formalization of new geometric and informational quantifier spaces, and advanced multi-modal or hybrid physics-informed formulations. Across these works, we observe a shared theme: blind adoption of massive generic architectures is giving way to tailored, structured, and resource-aware paradigms.

### 1. The Reality Check: Efficiency, Cost, and the Limits of Foundation Models
A dominant thread in recent evaluations is the critical reappraisal of heavy foundation models against classical or lightweight fitted alternatives. 

* **Industrial Monitoring Realities:** The empirical study evaluating Time-Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) like MOMENT-small, Chronos-T5, and TimesFM 2.5 against lightweight baselines (such as TCN-AE and OCSVM) reveals a stark reality. For industrial monitoring tasks (e.g., degradation-risk proxy detection), localized autoencoders and classic models dramatically outperform zero-shot or frozen TSFMs while demanding a fraction of the VRAM, latency, and storage footprint. TSFMs are increasingly positioned as task-dependent tools rather than universal drop-in solutions.
* **Agentic Few-Shot Harnesses:** Rather than deploying resource-heavy foundation models directly for forecasting, **MetaCaster** champions an agentic paradigm where LLM-driven agents act as intermediary engineering harnesses. By guiding data generation using text and a few examples, they automate the training of lightweight task-specific forecasters, striking a high balance of data and computational efficiency across 18 datasets.

### 2. Deepening the Theoretical Foundations of Time-Series
Several works seek to ground time-series and neural network behavior in rigorous mathematical and information-theoretic frameworks.

* **Informational Quantifier Spaces:** Moving beyond single-metric descriptions, the **mutual information-entropy plane** introduces a two-dimensional quantifier space combining normalized permutation entropy and normalized permutation mutual information. By integrating Bandt-Pompe embeddings with conditional entropy, this framework neatly characterizes regular, chaotic, and stochastic dynamics while explicitly resolving directional dependencies.
* **Convexity and Reflection Features in Neural Networks:** On the theoretical front, a fascinating look at deep neural networks in low dimensions proves that training piecewise linear networks on 1-D data is equivalent to solving convex Lasso problems with discrete dictionaries. While 2-layer networks leverage ramp functions, deeper architectures recursively generate *reflection features*, providing a closed-form window into global solution landscapes and sharpening our understanding of autoregressive time-series models.
* **Anytime-Valid Statistical Inference:** Addressing the limitations of static tests during structural breaks, the application of e-value-based sequential testing enables *anytime-valid* inference for probabilistic forecasting calibration. As demonstrated on international macroeconomic inflation forecasts, sequential testing successfully uncovers forecast misspecification during structural shifts that traditional full-sample tests entirely miss.

### 3. Hybrid Physics, Multimodality, and Advanced Retrieval
To capture complex real-world dynamics, recent architectures fuse diverse physical paradigms, modalities, or retrieve historical patterns via supervised relevance.

* **Quantum-Classical Physics-Informed Hybrids:** For chaotic partial differential equation (PDE) forecasting, the **QRC+PIC** framework pairs a pure-state quantum reservoir computer (to model latent coefficient dynamics) with a physics-informed local corrector (PIC). This proposal-correction mechanism successfully suppresses error accumulation in chaotic regimes like the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation.
* **Multimodal Long-Horizon Forecasting:** Recognizing that numerical trends often live alongside textual context, **Dual-Stream Attention (DSA)** introduces bidirectional cross-modal conditioning between numerical epidemiological signals and text headlines. Applied to long-horizon influenza forecasting, it demonstrates substantial error reductions over uni-modal transformer and LLM baselines.
* **Supervised Historical Retrieval:** In pattern-based time-series forecasting, naive similarity heuristics often fail. By leveraging realized futures as privileged training supervision, a novel two-stage retrieval framework decouples coarse candidate generation from a lightweight residual MLP reranker, establishing listwise future-compatibility targets that outperform classic retrieval protocols.

### 4. Specialized Oversampling and Diagnostic Diagnostics
* **Frequency-Domain Contrastive Oversampling:** To address severe class imbalance in time-series classification without falling into the trap of poor generative diversity, **FreMGP** leverages a contrastive learning-based frequency-domain class-discriminative representation module to guide multi-tree genetic programming.
* **Counterfactual Transition Graphs (CGT):** Diagnostic interpretability is pushed forward by CGT, a structural framework evaluating counterfactual transition quality in classifiers. By mapping classes as nodes and edge weights as CF reliability, CGT exposes how replacement-based and gradient-based explainers diverge from true classification boundaries and data manifolds.
