#### 1. High-Dimensional Time Series & Structural Decomposition
In statistical learning and econometrics, high-dimensional data often suffers from the curse of dimensionality, forcing a trade-off between parsimony and expressive capacity. 
* **The MARCF Framework (2503.05340):** Addressing matrix time series, the authors introduce the Matrix Autoregressive model with Common Factors (MARCF). By cleverly decomposing coefficient matrices into *common*, *predictor-specific*, and *response-specific* components, MARCF bridges matrix autoregression and matrix factor models. This structural factorization achieves effective dimension reduction while retaining the rich dynamics needed for global macroeconomic forecasting. Backed by a regularized gradient descent estimator with proven local linear convergence, it represents a robust advancement for multi-dimensional sequential data.
* **Astrophysical Transient Signals (2602.19214):** Shifting from macroeconomics to gamma-ray astrophysics, researchers investigated quasiperiodic oscillations (QPOs) in blazars using Fermi-LAT light curves. By employing Seasonal and Trend decomposition using Loess (STL) for detrending, the study revealed that most blazars exhibit transient QPO behavior rather than strict long-term periodicity. Crucially, the integration of **transformer-based deep learning** with traditional statistical forecasting over a four-year horizon demonstrates how modern sequence models are increasingly bridging the gap between descriptive time-series analysis and predictive astrophysics.

#### 2. Exact Quantum Signal Processing
In the realm of quantum computing, constraints on register sizes and circuit topologies often dictate algorithmic design. 
* **Quantum Bluestein’s Algorithm (2512.15349):** The standard Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) is traditionally efficient primarily for lengths that are powers of two, relying on zero-padding—which introduces spectral distortions—for arbitrary sizes. This paper introduces a quantum analogue of Bluestein's algorithm (QBA) to compute exact $N$-point QFTs for *arbitrary* sizes. By factoring the QFT unitary into three diagonal quadratic-phase gates and two radix-2 QFT subcircuits, QBA achieves an optimal asymptotic gate complexity of $O(N)$ using $O(\log N)$ qubits. Validated via Qiskit, this opens up cleaner pathways for quantum kernels and biomedical time-series analysis where data lengths rarely conform to powers of two.
