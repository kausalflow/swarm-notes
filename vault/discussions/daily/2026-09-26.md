### 1. Structural Priors and Inductive Biases in Neural Architectures
A recurring theme is the necessity of building structured mathematical constraints directly into models rather than relying purely on unconstrained deep learning.
* **Periodic and Relative Alignment:** In **ROOSTER** (2609.27473), the authors tackle multivariate forecasting and PPG-to-vital-sign reconstruction by abandoning heuristic content matching. Instead, they introduce a shared "periodic-comb bias" over target-condition offsets with learnable period and sharpness, proving that explicit relative alignment outperforms standard attention-based matching.
* **Dissipativity-by-Construction:** Moving into control systems, the work on **Dissipativity-by-Construction Discrete-Time Neural Networks** (2609.27188) avoids continuous-time ODE solvers altogether. By using constrained parameterizations, they guarantee incremental dissipativity and stability *by construction* for learning unknown dynamics from input-output time series—an essential safety property for robotics.
* **Non-Euclidean and Irregular Domains:** In astronomy, the **MCARMA** framework (2609.28419) addresses irregularly sampled, heteroscedastic multi-band time series via a multivariate continuous-time ARMA model, showing that joint multi-band inference vastly outperforms independent single-band fits. Similarly, **Kairos** (2609.27467) scales 3D scene graphs into 4D predictions for pedestrian flow, maintaining sparse memory efficiency while supporting continuous-time querying.

### 2. Spatiotemporal Scale and Physics Integration
Large-scale spatial networks and physical systems require tailored inductive biases to handle high-dimensional dependencies without catastrophic computational overhead.
* **Traffic and Climate Scaling:** For massive networks, **LoReST** (2609.27637) balances local neighborhood heterogeneity with long-range regional context in traffic forecasting using a decoupled spatial-temporal strategy. In atmospheric modeling, a **Pressure-Coordinate Thermodynamic PINN** (2609.27290) demonstrates that embedding physical advection-source equations yields massive accuracy gains under extreme observation sparsity (down to 5%), though it highlights a crucial limitation: complex terrain breaks fixed vertical coordinate assumptions.
* **High-Dimensional Latent Subspaces:** On the statistical side, a new **Model-Agnostic Noise Reduction Framework** (2609.27614) provides optimal linear projections using lagged covariance matrices and bootstrap dimension selection, achieving parametric convergence rates when recovering low-dimensional dynamic components from high-dimensional observational noise.

### 3. Agentic Workflows, Tool Evolution, and Operational Decision-Making
Language models and automated agents are increasingly moving from passive analysts to active decision-makers operating under real-world constraints.
* **Budgeted Forecast Workflows:** The **Forecast Workflow Bench (FWBench)** (2609.27385) evaluates how language-agent systems buy and utilize short-horizon forecasts and time-series tools under strict cost-loss optimization constraints. * **Failure-Driven Self-Evolution:** To address the brittleness of static tool libraries, **TimeEvo** (2609.27290) introduces a self-evolution framework where an agent diagnoses its own failures, clusters them into capability gaps, dynamically synthesizes new evidence-only tools, and validates them via a paired admission gate—allowing agents to bootstrap effective toolsets from scratch.

* **Speculative Vision Pipelines:** **MVP** (2609.27706) tackles vision system latency by pushing perception extrapolation directly into the motion domain via a lightweight ISP hardware extension, shifting full backend inference off the critical path as a non-blocking background task to save up to 46% energy.
* **Quantum Reservoir Computing:** Exploring non-classical substrates, researchers successfully simulated **Analog Neutral-Atom Quantum Reservoirs** (2609.28441), showing that controlled dissipation and operating at the edge of quantum chaos allow as few as five atoms to solve complex temporal tasks like the Mackey-Glass benchmark.
