### Daily Research Review: High-Dimensional Time Series & Stellar Dynamics

Today’s arXiv batch features a striking methodological divergence, bridging high-dimensional statistical physics/econometrics with precision astrophysics. While outwardly disparate—one dealing with fractal stochastic processes and the other with interacting binary stars—both papers tackle a fundamental challenge: **extracting hidden structural properties from complex, noisy observational systems through rigorous joint modeling.**

### 1. High-Dimensional Fractals and Spectral Clustering
**Paper:** *A Spectral Clustering-Type Algorithm for the Consistent Estimation of the Hurst Distribution in Moderately High Dimensions* (2501.18115)

* **The Problem:** Estimating memory parameters (the Hurst exponent, $H$) in high-dimensional time series is notoriously difficult due to curse-of-dimensionality issues and non-stationary noise. Traditional mixed-Gaussian modeling often fails to capture the true underlying heterogeneity of fractal systems.
* **The Approach:** The authors introduce a wavelet random matrix framework coupled with a provably convergent spectral clustering algorithm. Crucially, they solve the perennial hyperparameter tuning problem via an automated model selection procedure.
* **Why it Matters:** By establishing theoretical consistency in a joint asymptotic regime (where dimension, sample size, and scale scale simultaneously), this work moves spectral clustering from a heuristic tool to a rigorously grounded estimator for complex systems. Its immediate utility is demonstrated on macroeconomic time series, revealing non-trivial cointegration structures that standard techniques miss.

### 2. Precision Astrometry of Post-Mass-Transfer Binaries
**Paper:** *Full orbital solution and dynamical masses for a new shell-Be + sdOB binary AN Col* (2610.02367)

* **The Problem:** Be stars (rapidly rotating main-sequence stars with circumstellar disks) and their stripped subdwarf O/B (sdOB) companions are key to understanding binary mass transfer, yet very few systems have complete, model-independent mass measurements.
* **The Approach:** The study combines high-resolution near-infrared interferometry from the GRAVITY instrument with double-lined spectroscopy. A major technical hurdle is decoupling intrinsic, long-term disk-wide density oscillations (~5.1-year cycle) from the actual orbital motion (87.7-day period).
* **Why it Matters:** AN Col becomes only the second known Be + sdOB binary with a complete 3D orbital solution and parallax-independent dynamical masses ($8.39 M_\odot$ and $0.81 M_\odot$). This provides a crucial empirical anchor for constraining binary stellar evolution codes and theories of Be-disk formation.

### Synthesis: The Quest for Latent Coordinates
Though operating in vastly different domains, both papers share a common underlying philosophy: **signal separation in the presence of confounding internal dynamics.** 

* In **macroeconomics**, the challenge is separating multi-scale fractal persistence (Hurst distributions) from high-dimensional noise using spectral geometry.
* In **astrophysics**, the challenge is separating rapid orbital mechanics from slow, large-scale circumstellar disk oscillations using interferometric phase and spectroscopy.

Both contributions underscore the power of modern multi-modal data fusion—whether combining wavelet transforms with random matrix theory, or interferometry with spectroscopy—to unlock rigorous, low-dimensional truths from high-dimensional chaos.
