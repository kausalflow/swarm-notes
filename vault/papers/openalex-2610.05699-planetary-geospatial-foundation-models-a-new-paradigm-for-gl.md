## Summary

This paper presents Google Earth AI's Population Dynamics Foundation Model (PDFM), a planetary geospatial foundation model that encodes multimodal search, mobility, and environmental signals into generalizable place representations. Evaluated across four global health domains, five tasks, and four countries, PDFM successfully addresses critical surveillance gaps such as spatial extrapolation, nowcasting, and probabilistic forecasting. The findings demonstrate that geospatial foundation models can significantly enhance public health monitoring, outperforming traditional workflows affected by spatial gaps and temporal lags.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the Population Dynamics Foundation Model (PDFM) to encode multimodal search, mobility, and environmental signals into generalizable place representations for public health surveillance.
- Demonstrates PDFM across four global health domains (vaccine-preventable, communicable, noncommunicable, maternal mental health) and five tasks (spatial extrapolation, nowcasting, probabilistic forecasting, prospective forecasting, risk stratification).
- Evaluates performance across four countries (USA, Canada, Mexico, DRC), improving cross-border MMR vaccination coverage predictions, cardiovascular disease nowcasting, dengue forecasting, cholera hotspot identification, and unseen-state postpartum-depression risk stratification.

## Limitations

PDFM does not replace individual socioeconomic data or close demographic screening gaps in individual-level clinical tasks.
