# Comparing GNSS derived sea ice drift in the Arctic and Antarctic using rotary spectra and principal component analysis

**Authors**: James Hepworth, Amit Kumar Mishra
**Date**: 2026-09-01
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.18737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18737)

## Summary

This paper presents a cross-polar comparison of GNSS-tracked sea ice drift time series in the Arctic and Antarctic using rotary spectral analysis and principal component analysis. The study contrasts Arctic composites (2017–2024) with eight heterogeneous Antarctic campaigns (2000–2022) to evaluate air-sea ice coupling. Results reveal shared spectral organisation—such as strong low-frequency variance and near-inertial rotary enhancement—despite the Southern Ocean occupying a higher-energy amplitude envelope. These findings establish frequency-resolved benchmarks to inform future polar observing systems without assuming identical dynamical regimes.

## Key Contributions

- Presents a cross-polar comparison of GNSS-tracked sea ice drift time series between the Arctic (2017–2024 composites) and the Antarctic (eight heterogeneous campaigns, 2000–2022).
- Applies rotary spectral analysis and principal component analysis to reveal spectral organisation, identifying strong low-frequency variance and hemisphere-appropriate rotary enhancement near the Coriolis frequency in both regions.
- Demonstrates that while Arctic and Antarctic ice drift share similar spectral band structures, the Southern Ocean occupies a higher-energy envelope with distinct drift amplitudes.
- Provides transferable frequency-resolved benchmarks (dominant variance below 0.5 cpd and in the near-inertial band) to guide future observing-system design.

## Limitations

Antarctic data comprise heterogeneous campaigns with small buoy counts and short durations compared to the better-sampled Arctic.
