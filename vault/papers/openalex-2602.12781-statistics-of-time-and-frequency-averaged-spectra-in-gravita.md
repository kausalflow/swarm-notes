# Statistics of time and frequency-averaged spectra in gravitational-wave background searches

**Authors**: Quentin Baghi, Nikolaos Karnesis, Jean-Baptiste Bayle
**Date**: 2026-10-26
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2602.12781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12781)

## Summary

This paper investigates the validity of assuming uncorrelated time chunks and frequency bins in time series analysis for stochastic gravitational-wave background searches. The authors examine the consequences of time and frequency averaging, commonly used to reduce likelihood evaluation costs, and introduce an analytical Fisher information tool to quantify resulting parameter inference errors. Additionally, the study addresses locally stationary processes and provides insights into optimal time chunking strategies.

## Key Contributions

- Analyzed the validity of assuming uncorrelated time chunks or frequency bins in stochastic gravitational-wave background searches.
- Examined the impact of time and frequency averaging used to reduce computational expense in likelihood evaluations.
- Introduced an analytical Fisher information-based tool to quantify parameter inference errors caused by neglecting time and frequency correlation effects.
- Addressed locally stationary processes and established principles for optimal time chunking.
