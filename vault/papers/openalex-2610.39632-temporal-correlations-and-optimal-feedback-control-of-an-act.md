# Temporal Correlations and Optimal Feedback Control of an Active Information Engine

**Authors**: Laura Hoek, Yael Roichman
**Date**: 2026-10-29
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.39632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39632)

## Summary

This paper examines how temporal correlations affect feedback control and work extraction in a macroscopic active information engine driven by self-propelled bristle-robots. Using experimentally measured occupancy time series, the authors show that temporal correlations heavily influence vacancy detection and first-passage times. They demonstrate that while higher-order memory exists, a first-order Markov approximation is sufficient to design near-optimal control protocols and correctly capture the strategic shift toward sparse sensing under sensing costs.

## Key Contributions

- Investigates the role of temporal correlations in the optimal feedback control of a macroscopic active information engine driven by self-propelled bristle-robots.
- Demonstrates that first-order Markov approximations capture key vacancy detection and mean first-passage time characteristics, achieving within 0.15% of maximum experimental power despite higher-order temporal memory.
- Shows that incorporating sensing costs shifts optimal strategies toward sparse sensing, a shift captured by the Markov model but missed by independent measurement models.
