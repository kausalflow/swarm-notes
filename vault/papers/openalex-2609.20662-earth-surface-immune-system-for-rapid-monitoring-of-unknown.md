# Earth Surface Immune System for Rapid Monitoring of Unknown Anomalies

**Authors**: Jingtao Li, Qian Zhu, Xinyu Wang, Deren Li, Liangpei Zhang, Yanfei Zhong
**Date**: 2026-09-17
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2609.20662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20662)

## Summary

The paper introduces ESIA (Earth Surface Immune System), a bio-inspired framework for rapid monitoring and open-vocabulary recognition of unknown Earth surface anomalies using time-series satellite imagery. By combining a non-specific innate immune stage for binary localization, a specific adaptive immune stage using negative selection for text prompt filtering, and a test-time mutation mechanism for fine-tuning embeddings, ESIA bridges the gap between raw anomaly detection and actionable intelligence. Evaluated on a global-scale remote sensing dataset and real-world disaster scenarios, the system demonstrates high localization accuracy, fast processing speeds, and robust zero-shot adaptability to unforeseen environmental threats.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes ESIA (Earth Surface Immune System), inspired by biological immune principles to detect and recognize unknown Earth surface anomalies from time-series satellite imagery.
- Achieves a binary localization F1 score surpassing the strongest general baseline by 37% at 14.51 km2/s.
- Performs open-vocabulary recognition of unknown anomaly attributes (category, affected area, damage severity) with an F1 exceeding 80% using a negative selection and adaptive mutation mechanism.
- Validated on a global-scale dataset covering 19,801.60 km2 across six anomaly categories, and applied to real-world disaster events such as the Kakhovka Dam collapse and the Palisades Fire.
