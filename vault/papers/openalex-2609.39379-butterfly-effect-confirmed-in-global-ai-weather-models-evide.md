# Butterfly Effect Confirmed in Global AI Weather Models: Evidence from Tropical Cyclone Forecasting

**Authors**: Jeremy Cheuk‐Hin Leung, Daosheng Xu, Weiye Yu, Shaojing Zhang, Xiaodong Zeng, Gaozhen Nie, Jie Feng, Jingchen Pu, Li Yi, Kaijun Ren, Qingcun Zeng, Banglin Zhang, Banglin Zhang
**Date**: 2026-09-30
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2609.39379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39379)

## Summary

This study resolves a paradox in AI weather prediction by confirming that global AI weather models indeed simulate the atmospheric butterfly effect. Through the analysis of Super Typhoon Khanun, the authors demonstrate that minor initial perturbations in specific regions trigger state transitions within a double-attractor system, leading to substantial forecast divergence. This chaotic behavior aligns with numerical weather prediction models and is observed in about 12% of recent tropical cyclones, validating AI models' capacity to capture atmospheric chaos.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that global AI weather prediction models exhibit the atmospheric butterfly effect via counterexamples in tropical cyclone forecasting.
- Reveals that for Super Typhoon Khanun, AI predictions are constrained by a double-attractor system where minor initial perturbations trigger state transitions causing large positional divergences.
- Shows that this chaotic attractor-switching behavior is consistent with numerical weather prediction models and occurs in approximately 12% of tropical cyclones over the past 5 years.
