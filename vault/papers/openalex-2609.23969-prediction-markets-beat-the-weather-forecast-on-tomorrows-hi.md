# Prediction Markets Beat the Weather Forecast on Tomorrow's High Temperature

**Authors**: Alexander W. Crosier
**Date**: 2026-09-21
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2609.23969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23969)

## Summary

This paper evaluates the performance of prediction markets on the Kalshi exchange for forecasting tomorrow's high temperature across seven US cities over five years. By extracting hour-by-hour market-implied forecasts, the study reveals that prediction markets outperform leading public meteorological models such as the National Blend of Models (NBM) in root-mean-square error. Furthermore, analysis of forecast trajectories shows that public models update toward market prices rather than vice versa, highlighting the efficacy of prediction markets in aggregating dispersed information rapidly.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that prediction markets on the Kalshi exchange beat the leading National Blend of Models (NBM) weather forecast in six out of seven US cities for tomorrow's high temperature.
- Shows that prediction markets achieve about a 10 percent improvement in root-mean-square error over the best single public forecasting product after the first hour of trading.
- Reveals that public weather forecasts update toward the market price rather than the market reacting to weather forecast releases, indicating that prediction markets aggregate dispersed temperature information faster than public systems.

## Limitations

Limited to high temperature predictions across seven American cities over a five-year period on a single prediction market exchange (Kalshi).
