# The JWST weather report: Unravelling the atmospheric variability of isolated worlds using principal component analysis

**Authors**: Merle A. Schrader, Johanna M. Vos, E. Nasedkin, Jennifer Kestell, Nicolas B. Cowan, Roman Akhmetshyn, Samuel Beiler, Beth A. Biller, Ben Burningham, Jacqueline Faherty, Eileen C. Gonzales, Allison M. McCarthy, Caroline V. Morley, Barry O'Donovan, Cian O'Toole, Genaro Suárez, Xianyu Tan, Channon Visscher, Niall Whiteford, Yifan Zhou
**Date**: 2026-07-31
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2607.26182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26182)

## Summary

This paper applies principal component analysis (PCA) to JWST time-resolved spectroscopy of the planetary-mass brown dwarf SIMP J013656.5+093347 to investigate its atmospheric variability. The authors find that the observed spectral variability is intrinsically low dimensional, where two principal components account for all coherent variations down to the noise floor. These components correspond to temperature changes and vertical cloud structure, successfully mapping the rotational phase evolution into linear combinations of three distinct atmospheric states.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that the rotational spectroscopic variability of brown dwarf SIMP J013656.5+093347 is intrinsically low-dimensional, with two principal components capturing all detectable coherent variability down to the noise floor.
- Identifies that the leading principal component captures broadband temperature variations while the second captures chromatic variability driven by vertical cloud structure.
- Establishes a model-independent principal component analysis framework to map rotational phase contributions of three extreme atmospheric states in substellar and exoplanetary atmospheres.
