# The Effect of Field Line Tracing Height on Wang‐Sheeley‐Arge Solar Wind Predictions at Earth

**Authors**: Satabdwa Majumdar, Martin Reiß, Pete Riley, Charles Nickolos Arge
**Date**: 2026-11-01
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.28274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28274)

## Summary

This paper investigates the impact of the magnetic field line tracing height on solar wind predictions generated by the Wang-Sheeley-Arge model at Earth. By raising the tracing height slightly above the photosphere, the authors mitigate the disruptive effects of small closed field regions inside coronal holes, yielding smoother open-field boundaries. Evaluation across 53 Carrington rotations reveals that a higher tracing height significantly improves the accuracy of high-speed enhancement start times, durations, and peak speeds while reducing false alarms.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that raising the magnetic field line tracing height in the Wang-Sheeley-Arge model improves solar wind high-speed enhancement (HSE) predictions across 53 Carrington rotations.
- Shows that a higher tracing height produces smoother open-field boundaries and reduces small-scale fragmentation within coronal holes, leading to more stable footpoint mappings and robust great-circle angular distance calculations.
- Establishes that tuning the tracing height enhances forecasting performance while simultaneously reducing false alarms in ambient solar wind modeling.
