## Summary

This paper presents high signal-to-noise, medium-resolution time-series JWST/NIRSpec spectra of WISE 0855, the coldest known brown dwarf at 265K. By analyzing 11 hours of observations covering 2.87-5.27 $\mu$m, the authors find that carbon monoxide gas absorption exhibits up to 10% peak-to-peak modulations. Principal component analysis reveals correlations between carbon monoxide and phosphine variations, while comparisons with atmospheric models suggest patchy water clouds and distinct pressure layers governing chemical abundances versus cloud thickness.

## Key Contributions

- Presented high signal-to-noise, medium-resolution JWST/NIRSpec time-series spectra of the coldest known brown dwarf, WISE 0855 (265K).
- Discovered that carbon monoxide gas absorption exhibits time-variable modulations with a peak-to-peak amplitude up to 10%.
- Demonstrated using principal component analysis that variations in carbon monoxide and phosphine correlate with one another.
- Provided evidence of patchy water clouds and constrained that chemical abundance variations originate from quenched pressures while water cloud thickness variations occur at lower pressures.
