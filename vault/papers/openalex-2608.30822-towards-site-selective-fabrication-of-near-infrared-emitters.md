# Towards Site-Selective Fabrication of Near-Infrared Emitters in hBN

**Authors**: Tadas Paulauskas, Vakaris Šilys, Edgaras Markauskas, J. Janušonis, Ifra Bibi, Skirmantas Keršulis, Danielis Rutkauskas, M. Maciaszek
**Date**: 2026-08-31
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.30822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30822)

## Summary

This paper investigates the site-selective fabrication of near-infrared (NIR) emitters in hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) by combining single-shot femtosecond laser writing with oxygen-rich annealing. The proposed method enables spatial control over oxygen-related NIR activation at predefined coordinates, producing narrow multipeak emissions extending up to 1 um with single-photon emission signatures. The findings demonstrate effective deterministic emitter activation while highlighting remaining challenges such as spectral multiplicity and off-site activation.

## Key Contributions

- Combined single-shot femtosecond laser writing with oxygen-rich annealing to achieve site-selective activation of near-infrared (NIR) emitters in hexagonal boron nitride (hBN).
- Demonstrated narrow and multipeak NIR emission extending up to 1 um, with over 83% of spectra under 785 nm excitation and 71% under 635 nm excitation containing resolved peaks above 810 nm at written sites.
- Showed monotonically increasing emission intensity with writing-pulse energy and confirmed single-photon emission characteristics via band-pass-resolved zero-delay correlation dips ($g^{(2)}(0)$ values).

## Limitations

Identified residual off-site activation and spectral multiplicity as principal targets for further optimization.
