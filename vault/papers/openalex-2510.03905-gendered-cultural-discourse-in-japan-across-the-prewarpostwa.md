# Gendered cultural discourse in Japan across the prewar–postwar transition: evidence from historical word embeddings

**Authors**: Shintaro Sakai, Haewoon Kwak, Jisun An, Akira Matsui
**Date**: 2026-08-18
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2510.03905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03905)

## Summary

This paper quantifies the evolution of gender stereotypes in Japan between 1900 and 1998 using historical word embeddings trained on yearly text corpora. By measuring cosine similarity differences relative to male and female attribute words across the Home, Work, and Politics domains, the authors reveal that female associations in public domains (Work and Politics) shifted significantly from negative prewar trends to positive postwar trends around 1945. Meanwhile, the Home domain remained stable, and occupation-level stereotype values showed a moderate correlation with actual historical demographic participation.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies the evolution of gender stereotypes in Japan from 1900 to 1998 using yearly historical word embeddings across three domains: Home, Work, and Politics.
- Reveals domain-specific trend reversals around 1945 in Work and Politics, shifting from negative prewar trends to positive postwar trends following institutional transformations under the Allied Occupation.
- Demonstrates that female stereotype values for 18 occupations exhibit a moderately positive correlation (r=0.364) with actual demographic gender participation statistics.
