# Deformation of the CME and CME-driven shock due to interaction with the ambient solar wind: I. Modelling with Cone Model

**Authors**: A. Niemela, A. Valentino, J. Magdalenic
**Date**: 2026-08-03
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.01621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01621)

## Summary

This paper investigates the spatial and temporal deformation of Coronal Mass Ejections (CMEs) and CME-driven shock waves interacting with the ambient solar wind using the 3D MHD model EUHFORIA and the cone CME model. By examining two Earth-directed events from December 2020 and October 2021, the authors demonstrate that background solar wind structure induces significant structural and gas compression changes. Their findings reveal that flank encounters can experience arrival time discrepancies of up to 16 hours, highlighting critical implications for space weather forecasting.

## Key Contributions

- Analyzed the deformation of two Earth-directed CMEs (Dec 7, 2020 and Oct 28, 2021) and their driven shocks using the EUHFORIA 3D MHD model and cone CME model.
- Demonstrated that ambient solar wind interactions cause significant spatial and temporal deformations of CMEs and shocks, varying substantially based on background wind complexity.
- Showed that CME flank encounters at Earth can exhibit arrival time differences of up to 16 hours due to solar wind interactions, impacting space weather forecasting accuracy.
