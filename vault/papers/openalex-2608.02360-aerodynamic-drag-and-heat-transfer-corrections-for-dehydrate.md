# Aerodynamic Drag and Heat Transfer Corrections for Dehydrated Pollen Particles: CFD-Based Modeling of Airborne Allergen Transport in Smart Urban Environments

**Authors**: Omar Hamad, Samer Ali, Mahmoud M. khaled, Talib Dbouk
**Date**: 2026-08-03
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.02360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02360)

## Summary

This paper presents Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations investigating the aerodynamic drag and convective heat transfer of realistically dehydrated pollen particles across Reynolds numbers from 0.1 to 15. The findings show that dry pollen particles exhibit 8% to 15% higher drag coefficients and 5% to 15% lower Nusselt numbers compared to conventional hydrated spherical assumptions. These results demonstrate that standard spherical correlations are inadequate for accurately simulating airborne allergen transport and evaporation in urban environments.

## Key Contributions

- Provides advanced CFD simulations evaluating aerodynamic drag forces and convective heat transfer of realistically dehydrated pollen particles across Reynolds numbers from 0.1 to 15.
- Reveals that dry pollen particles exhibit drag coefficients 8% to 15% higher than hydrated spherical particles.
- Shows that Nusselt numbers for dry pollen particles are 5% to 15% lower than those for hydrated pollen particles, demonstrating the inadequacy of conventional spherical correlations.

## Limitations

Limited to particle Reynolds numbers between 0.1 and 15 corresponding to atmospheric wind speeds from 0.27 to 30 km/h.
