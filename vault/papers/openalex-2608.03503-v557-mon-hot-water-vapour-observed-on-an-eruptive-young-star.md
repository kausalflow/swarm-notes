## Summary

This paper presents multi-epoch photometric and spectroscopic observations of V557 Mon, an eruptive young star in the Rosette Nebula that experienced a year-long EXor outburst. By tracking molecular emission features such as hot water vapour, TiO, VO, and CO using ExoMol models, the authors trace the evolution of the inner accretion disk, revealing that inner-disk temperatures dropped from 3000 K to 2000 K during the fading phase and correlated positively with stellar brightness.

## Key Contributions

- Presented multi-band photometric time series and multi-epoch optical/near-infrared spectra capturing a year-long EXor outburst of the young stellar object V557 Mon.
- Determined a peak accretion rate of 6.3x10^(-7) M_sol/yr during the outburst (about 70 times higher than quiescence) and tracked the inner-disk temperature decreasing from 3000 K to 2000 K using ExoMol molecular emission modeling.
- Discovered transient hot water vapour emission features alongside TiO, VO, and CO, revealing a positive correlation between molecular excitation temperature and stellar brightness.
