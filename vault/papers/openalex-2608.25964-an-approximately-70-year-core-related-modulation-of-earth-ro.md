# An approximately 70-year core-related modulation of Earth rotation and its implications for the leap second

**Authors**: Zewen Zhang, Yuanwei Wu, Xishun Li, Dang Yao, Xuan Cheng, Xuhai Yang, Shougang Zhang
**Date**: 2026-09-28
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.25964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25964)

## Summary

Recent observations of Earth's rotational acceleration and length of day (LOD) fluctuations are examined to determine their consistency with a core-related ~70-year modulation. By analyzing residual LOD and core angular momentum (CAM) over the 1883-2022 interval using harmonic regression and spectral analysis, the study reveals robust ~70-year periodicities and high correlations between core dynamics and rotational variability. The results indicate that this multidecadal component alone does not necessitate a negative leap second in the near term.

## Key Contributions

- Identified a near-70-year component in residual length of day (LOD) and core angular momentum (CAM) over the 1883-2022 interval, revealing strong zero-lag (0.918) and lagged (0.932 at 2-year CAM lead) correlations.
- Demonstrated via harmonic regression and Lomb-Scargle spectra that periods of 69.7 yr (residual LOD) and 71.8 yr (CAM-derived equivalent LOD) characterize low-frequency Earth rotation modulation.
- Provided a model-dependent geophysical assessment showing that the multidecadal component alone does not indicate a sustained near-term shortening of the day requiring a negative leap second under the current framework.

## Limitations

The analysis cannot uniquely separate electromagnetic, topographic, gravitational, and viscous core-mantle coupling mechanisms, and the findings represent a model-dependent geophysical assessment rather than an operational prediction of future UTC adjustments.
