# Deep Learning in Infant Functional Neuroimaging: Challenges, Advances, and Future Directions

**Authors**: Dan Hu, Jiale Cheng, Weiran Xia, Kangfu Han, Matthew Wu, Li Wang, Weili Lin, Gang Li
**Date**: 2026-09-22
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2609.26688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26688)

## Summary

This paper reviews the application of deep learning to infant functional neuroimaging (fMRI), highlighting how advanced neural architectures overcome challenges such as short scan durations, structured motion artifacts, and rapid brain maturation. The authors synthesize recent methodological progress across input representation, individualized brain mapping, longitudinal trajectory forecasting, and biological translation, charting a shift from descriptive group-level analyses to reliable individual-level models. Finally, they outline future directions centered on larger longitudinal datasets, standardized evaluation, and clinical risk assessment.

## Key Contributions

- Synthesizes recent deep learning advances across input representation formatting, population and individualized brain mapping, and longitudinal trajectory forecasting for infant fMRI.
- Examines critical challenges in infant functional neuroimaging including short scan durations, structured motion artifacts, and rapid brain maturation.
- Outlines key future directions for bridging computational deep learning predictions with biological mechanisms and clinical neurodevelopmental risk assessment.

## Limitations

Future progress relies heavily on larger, more diverse longitudinal datasets and more developmentally appropriate model architectures.
