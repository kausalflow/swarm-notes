# Magnetic Activity as a Driver of Low-gravity-like Spectral Morphology in Late-M Dwarfs

**Authors**: Chia-Lung Lin, Dániel Apai, Benjamin V. Rackham, Prajwal Niraula, M. S. Giampapa, Ana Glidden, Julien de Wit, Valeriy Vasilyev, Н. М. Костогрыз, Alexander Shapiro
**Date**: 2026-10-22
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.31647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31647)

## Summary

This paper investigates why late-M dwarfs frequently exhibit low-surface-gravity-like near-infrared spectral morphologies, analyzing observational data from JWST, IRTF/SpeX, K2, and TESS. The authors find that retrieved surface gravities strongly anti-correlate with magnetic activity indicators such as flare and X-ray luminosities. Radiative-transfer modeling demonstrates that persistent small-scale magnetic activity alters the temperature-pressure structure of stellar photospheres, mimicking lower-gravity spectra.

## Key Contributions

- Investigated the connection between magnetic activity and low-surface-gravity-like near-infrared spectral morphology across late-M dwarfs using JWST NIRISS/SOSS, IRTF/SpeX, and space telescope flare observations.
- Demonstrated strong anti-correlations between retrieved surface gravities and flare/X-ray luminosities (Pearson/Spearman coefficients < -0.9, p <= 1.0x10^-4), indicating that magnetically active stars favor lower-gravity spectral morphologies.
- Showed via radiative-transfer experiments that activity-associated heating can produce lower-gravity-like spectral features without actual changes in physical surface gravity.
