# The James Webb Space Telescope Absolute Flux Calibration. VI. Near-infrared Camera Imaging and Coronagraphy

**Authors**: Martha L. Boyer, Ben Sunnquist, B. Hilbert, Dan Coe, Varun Bajaj, Paul Bennet, Julien H. Girard
**Date**: 2026-10-08
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.08717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08717)

## Summary

This paper presents an updated absolute flux calibration for all imaging modes, coronagraphic setups, and weak lenses of JWST's Near Infrared Camera (NIRCam), utilizing 3.5 years of observations across 19 standard stars. The calibration converts instrumental units into physical surface brightness units (MJy sr−1), accounts for subarray-to-full-frame count-rate discrepancies of up to 1%, and achieves a typical scatter of under 2% across filter and detector combinations. Furthermore, the analysis demonstrates exceptional long-term stability of the NIRCam detectors with minimal annual drift.

## Key Contributions

- Presents an updated absolute flux calibration for all imaging modes of JWST NIRCam, covering 29 filters, 5 coronagraphic setups, and weak lenses using 3.5 years of flux standard star observations.
- Incorporates subarray-to-full-frame count-rate differences of up to 1% into the calibration factors.
- Demonstrates high temporal stability of NIRCam detectors with potential count-rate decreases under 0.4% per year, and residual detector offsets typically under 1% (reaching up to 4%–5% in select filters).
