# V844 Herculis: An Exceptional Intermediate Polar Hiding in an Ordinary Dwarf Nova

**Authors**: Anousha Greiveldinger, Peter Garnavich, Colin Littlefield, Mark R. Kennedy, Paula Szkody, Jordan Tweddale, M. Whittle, Teófilo Arranz, Donald Collins, James DeYoung, Sjoerd Dufoer, Charles Galdies, Jean-François Gout, Franz-Josef Hambsch, E. Kardasis, David Messier, Arto Oksanen, G. Poyner, Richard Sabo
**Date**: 2026-09-22
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2609.26530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26530)

## Summary

This paper presents time-series spectroscopy and multi-wavelength observations of the SU UMa-type dwarf nova V844 Her, revealing it to be an intermediate polar with an exceptionally short binary period of 1.31 hours and a white dwarf spin period of 29.3 minutes. Through extensive optical, ultraviolet, and X-ray tracking during its 2023 superoutburst, the authors observe a drop in the X-ray count rate and propose it is caused by soft X-ray obscuration from an outbursting accretion disk.

## Key Contributions

- Discovered high-velocity Balmer and He I emission features modulated at a 29.3-minute period through time-series spectroscopy of V844 Her during quiescence.
- Identified V844 Her as an intermediate polar with the shortest binary period (1.31 hours) and one of the smallest orbit-to-spin ratios among confirmed IPs.
- Analyzed extensive optical, ultraviolet, and X-ray observations during the 2023 superoutburst, proposing that decreased X-ray count rates stem from soft X-ray absorption by an expanded outbursting disk.
