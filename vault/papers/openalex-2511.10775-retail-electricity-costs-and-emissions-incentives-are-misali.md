# Retail Electricity Costs and Emissions Incentives Are Misaligned for Commercial and Industrial Power Consumers

**Authors**: Fletcher Chapin, Akshay K. Rao, Adhithyan Sakthivelu, Carson I. Tucker, Eres David, Casey S. Chen, Erin Musabandesu, Meagan S. Mauter
**Date**: 2026-08-18
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2511.10775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10775)

## Summary

This paper investigates the economic and environmental implications of commercial and industrial load growth in the U.S. by compiling a comprehensive spatiotemporal dataset of retail electricity tariffs and demand response programs. By resolving data fragmentation and geospatial heterogeneity, the authors reconcile these economic data with marginal and average emission factors as well as market prices. Their analysis reveals significant spatiotemporal variation in energy flexibility incentives and uncovers a widespread misalignment between retail electricity costs and carbon emission incentives under current tariff structures.

## Key Contributions

- Presents a comprehensive spatiotemporal data set of U.S. price-based and incentive-based demand response for large commercial and industrial consumers.
- Resolves structural incompatibility and fragmentation of heterogeneous energy datasets using time series approximations and geospatial unification.
- Demonstrates significant spatial and temporal heterogeneity in cost and carbon emissions incentives for demand-side energy flexibility.
- Reveals broad structural misalignment between economic and carbon emission incentives under existing electricity tariffs, showing that current structures frequently incentivize more carbon-intensive consumption.
