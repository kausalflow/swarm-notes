# Disentangling the assembly of the central and diffuse stellar components in galaxy clusters through dynamical friction and tidal stripping

**Authors**: Frazer R. Pearce, S. P. Bamford, Garreth Martin, Yannick M Bahé, Harley J Brown, Madeline Butler, Ana Contreras-Santos, Weiguang Cui, N. A. Hatch, Alexander Knebe
**Date**: 2026-10-05
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.06660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06660)

## Summary

This paper presents a dynamically motivated definition to distinguish between the brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) and intracluster light (ICL) in galaxy clusters. Using zoom-in simulations from the Three Hundred project, the authors trace stellar assembly histories via hindcasting and forecasting methods, showing that BCGs form from the central galaxies of a few massive infalling systems via dynamical friction, while ICL consists of tidally stripped stars from all infalling systems. The resulting physically defined components occupy distinct regions of phase space while remaining spatially interleaved.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a dynamically motivated definition for distinguishing brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) from intracluster light (ICL) based on stellar assembly origins.
- Demonstrates through 107 zoom simulations that BCG stellar mass originates predominantly from the central galaxies of 3 to 5 massive infalling systems delivered via efficient dynamical friction.
- Develops a forecasting method to identify BCG and ICL components via the dynamical fates of originating systems across 261 clusters, showing distinct phase-space separation despite spatial interleaving.
