# Mixed Uncertainty in One View: Co-Visualizing Statistical Variability and Qualitative Confidence

**Authors**: Racquel Fygenson, Lace Padilla, Laura E. Matzen
**Date**: 2026-08-06
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.05487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05487)

## Summary

This paper investigates how to effectively co-visualize quantitative statistical variability and qualitative confidence in time-series forecasting line charts. Through three preregistered human-subjects studies comprising 923 participants, the authors evaluate various visualization methods, including separate textual and iconic representations as well as integrated visual encodings like color, transparency, and blurred strokes. The findings indicate that several non-textual encoding techniques can alter non-experts' decision-making and successfully convey subjective qualitative confidence alongside traditional confidence intervals.

## Key Contributions

- Investigates co-visualizing quantitative statistical variability and qualitative confidence in time-series forecasting line charts across three preregistered human-subjects studies (total n = 923).
- Evaluates separate (text, icons) and integrated (color, transparency, blurred stroke) visualization techniques for qualitative uncertainty.
- Demonstrates that non-textual encoding techniques can successfully help non-experts incorporate qualitative confidence into their forecasting decisions.
