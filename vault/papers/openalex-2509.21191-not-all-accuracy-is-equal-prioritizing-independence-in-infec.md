# Not all accuracy is equal: Prioritizing independence in infectious disease forecasting

**Authors**: Carson Dudley, Marisa C. Eisenberg
**Date**: 2026-09-09
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2509.21191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21191)

## Summary

Ensemble forecasts are a cornerstone of public health decision making, yet they often fail to achieve significant accuracy gains over individual models because constituent models make highly correlated errors. This paper examines COVID-19 and influenza forecasting models to show that current development practices prioritize stand-alone accuracy over diverse contributions, leading to high redundancy. To address this, the authors propose a framework and methodology for model fitting and ensemble construction that prioritize model independence and complementary information, thereby improving robustness for epidemic preparedness.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that highly correlated errors among constituent models limit the accuracy gains of disease forecasting ensembles during epidemics like COVID-19 and influenza.
- Analyzes residuals from COVID-19 forecasting models to reveal high redundancy and shared model failures stemming from optimization for stand-alone accuracy rather than ensemble contribution.
- Proposes a framework and improvements to model fitting and ensemble construction that prioritize model independence and reward unique information to improve forecast robustness.
