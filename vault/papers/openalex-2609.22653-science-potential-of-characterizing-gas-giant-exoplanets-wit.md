# Science potential of characterizing gas giant exoplanets with HWO

**Authors**: Beck Dacus, Jean-Baptiste Ruffio, Quinn Konopacky, Tyler Robinson, Mary Anne Limbach, Kielan K. Hoch, Katelyn Horstman, Dimitri P. Mawet, Michael W. McElwain, Bertrand P. Mennesson, Marshall Perrin, Laurent Pueyo, Dmitry Savransky, Corey Spohn, Sarah Steiger, Connor Vancil, Ji Wang, Nicole Wolff, Shelley Wright
**Date**: 2026-10-24
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2609.22653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22653)

## Summary

This paper evaluates the science potential of the upcoming Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO) for characterizing nearby gas giant exoplanets and searching for exomoons using the EXOSIMS direct imaging mission simulator. The authors demonstrate that HWO can achieve high signal-to-noise detections, resolve atmospheric absorption features of water, methane, and ammonia, and potentially detect rotation-induced variability and exomoon eclipses or biosignatures.

## Key Contributions

- Evaluated HWO observing capabilities for gas giant exoplanets using the EXOSIMS direct imaging mission simulator across 164 ExEP target stars.
- Demonstrated that HWO can achieve S/N >= 5 broadband visible-light detections within 5 minutes of integration for Jupiter-radius gas giants.
- Showed that 10 hours of R=1000 near-IR spectroscopy can reveal water, methane, and ammonia absorption features in gas giant atmospheres at Jupiter-like instellations across ExEP targets.
- Found that optical time-series photometry can exceed 1% flux precision, enabling the detection of rotation-induced variability and Mars-sized exomoon eclipses.

## Limitations

Future observational studies will need to account for more detailed instrument noise models and stellar activity effects.
