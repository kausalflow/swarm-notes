# Phase-curve approach to study atmospheric flows in hot Jupiters

**Authors**: D. Shulyak, W. Dietrich, V. Parmentier, D. Cont, L. -M. Lara, L. Gkouvelis, M. R. Swain, M. Rengel
**Date**: 2026-08-04
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.03406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03406)

## Summary

This paper investigates how multiwavelength phase curves can probe atmospheric circulation and vertical flows in hot Jupiters as a function of altitude. Using general circulation models and radiative transfer codes, the authors simulate observations across various space missions and evaluate filter sensitivities. The results reveal that atmospheric temperature and metallicity regulate phase-curve offsets, while NIRCam filters and high-resolution spectroscopy offer promising pathways to resolve vertical atmospheric flows.

## Key Contributions

- Utilized ADAM/GCM and radiative transfer models to predict multiwavelength phase curves for hot Jupiters across Spitzer, TESS, CHEOPS, HST, and JWST missions.
- Demonstrated that atmospheric temperature primarily regulates phase-curve offsets, while high metallicity reduces them.
- Identified NIRCam filters as providing sensitivity across a wide pressure range (10 bar to 1e-4 bar) for studying atmospheric flows vertically.

## Limitations

Discrepancies between model predictions and available observations indicate potential missing physical processes in current atmospheric models.
