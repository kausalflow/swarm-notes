# The Hill sphere transits of Beta Pictoris c and the search for another 1981-like event

**Authors**: V.E.C. Bakker, M. A. Kenworthy, R. Stuik, K. Zwintz, T. Guillot, S. Vach, J. J. Wang, J. Kammerer, W. Balmer, S. Lacour, S. Zieba, E. E. Mamajek, G. -D. Marleau, R.B. Kuhn, P. Kalas, Y. De Pra, E. J. W. de Mooij, N. Crouzet, M. Buttu, J. I. Bailey
**Date**: 2026-09-02
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2609.02708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02708)

## Summary

The authors investigate the origin of a historical 4% photometric fluctuation observed towards Beta Pictoris in 1981 by searching for analogous events and circumplanetary material transits in multi-epoch photometry from 2017 to 2023. Using data from BRITE, bRing, and ASTEP, they fit models of the 1981 event and flat circumplanetary disks during Hill sphere transits of Beta Pictoris c. No compelling evidence of a 1981-like event was found during primary transits, ruling out the hypothesis that the historical event originated from the Hill spheres of planets b and c while placing an upper limit of ~10^22 grams on the dust content of Beta Pictoris c's Hill sphere.

## Key Contributions

- Analyzed multi-epoch photometry from 2017 to 2023 (BRITE, bRing, ASTEP) to search for analogues to the 1981 photometric fluctuation event in the Beta Pictoris system.
- Fitted photometric observations to a flat disk model to search for signs of circumplanetary material during Hill sphere transits of Beta Pictoris c.
- Ruled out the hypothesis that the 1981 photometric event was related to the Hill spheres of planets Beta Pictoris b and c, placing an upper limit of ~10^22 grams of dust material in the Hill sphere of Beta Pictoris c.
