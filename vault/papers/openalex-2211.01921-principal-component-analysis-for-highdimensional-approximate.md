# Principal Component Analysis for High‐Dimensional Approximate Factor Models in Time Series: Assumptions, Asymptotic Theory, and Identification

**Authors**: Matteo Barigozzi
**Date**: 2026-09-29
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2211.01921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01921)

## Summary

This paper reviews the theoretical foundations of estimating large approximate factor models in high-dimensional stationary time series panels using Principal Component Analysis (PCA). It synthesizes conditions for consistency and asymptotic normality as both the cross-sectional dimension and sample size approach infinity. Special attention is dedicated to identification issues, proving that common and idiosyncratic components are identifiable only asymptotically and detailing the restrictions needed to uniquely identify factors and loadings for statistical inference.

## Key Contributions

- Reviews key theoretical conditions for consistency and asymptotic normality of Principal Component Analysis estimators in high-dimensional approximate factor models for stationary time series.
- Demonstrates that common and idiosyncratic components are identified only in the limit as cross-sectional and sample dimensions diverge.
- Examines restrictions required for unique determination of factors and loadings and analyzes their implications for statistical inference.
