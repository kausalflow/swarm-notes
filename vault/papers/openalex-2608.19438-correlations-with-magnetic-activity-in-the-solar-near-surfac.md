# Correlations with Magnetic Activity in the Solar Near-surface Shear Layer. I. Rotation

**Authors**: M. Cristina Rabello Soares, Sarbani Basu, R. S. Bogart
**Date**: 2026-09-22
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.19438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19438)

## Summary

This paper uses Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager data and ring-diagram analysis to examine the rotation rate and temporal variations of the solar near-surface shear layer between depths of 1 and 17 Mm. The authors find that the rotation rate exhibits a torsional oscillation pattern even at a shallow depth of 1 Mm, and that cumulative zonal displacement correlates with magnetic activity with multiyear lags depending on latitude. Furthermore, the results reveal pronounced hemispheric asymmetries in rotation rates, cumulative zonal displacements, and the temporal lags between flow and magnetic activity.

## Key Contributions

- Utilized Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager data and ring-diagram analysis to determine the rotation rate and time variation of the solar near-surface shear layer between 1 and 17 Mm depths.
- Demonstrated that the rotation rate shows the typical torsional oscillation pattern even at depths as shallow as 1 Mm.
- Identified significant correlations between cumulative zonal displacement and magnetic activity with multiyear lags, showing that displacement leads activity by ~5 yr near 15 degrees, while activity leads by ~4 yr at higher latitudes.

## Limitations

Longer time series are needed to test cycle-to-cycle variations and resolve hemispheric timing differences.
