# Medical world models in healthcare: foundations, applications, and challenges for trustworthy clinical translation

**Authors**: Zhaoyan Chen, Zhongxiu Cong, Zhuanfeng Jin, Wanshu Fan, Dongsheng Zhou, Qi Ai, Haifan Gong, Congyu Liao, Xiaofeng Liu, Cong Wang
**Date**: 2026-07-28
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2607.25242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25242)

## Summary

This review examines the foundations, applications, and translational challenges of medical world models, which extend AI beyond static prediction by simulating evolving patient states and treatment responses. Through a systematic synthesis of 98 sources and 14 empirical studies, the authors categorise the field into four core capabilities—state representation, temporal dynamics, intervention-conditioned simulation, and planning—while highlighting major hurdles such as causal identifiability, error accumulation, and the need for prospective multicentre validation.

## Key Contributions

- Presents a structured narrative synthesis and systematic evidence mapping of medical world models, screening 1,455 records to identify 98 sources including 14 studies meeting strict empirical criteria.
- Organises the field around four core capabilities: patient state representation, temporal dynamics modelling, intervention-conditioned simulation, and clinician-supervised planning.
- Identifies critical barriers to clinical translation, including inconsistent action semantics, limited causal identifiability, and long-horizon error accumulation, while outlining evidence requirements for trustworthy deployment.

## Limitations

Most existing studies remain retrospective, task-specific, or preclinical, constrained by incomplete longitudinal intervention data, inadequate uncertainty estimation, and limited external validation.
