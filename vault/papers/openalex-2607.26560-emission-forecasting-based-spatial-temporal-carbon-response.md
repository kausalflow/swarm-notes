# Emission-Forecasting-Based Spatial-Temporal Carbon Response: A Multi-Agent Attention-Enhanced Deep Learning Framework

**Authors**: Feiyu Cai, Jing Qiu, Yi Yang, Chenxi Zhang, Xinlei Wang, B. C. Liu, Junhua Zhao
**Date**: 2026-07-29
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2607.26560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26560)

## Summary

This paper introduces a proactive ex-ante spatial-temporal carbon response framework to overcome the latency of traditional ex-post nodal carbon intensity calculations in power systems. The core methodology couples a hierarchical deep learning design with a dual-stage attention mechanism and an LLM-based multi-agent system to accurately forecast day-ahead carbon intensity under renewable uncertainty. On the demand side, a spatial-temporal scheduling model incorporates geographically dispatchable loads like mobile energy storage and distributed data centers to rapidly respond to carbon fluctuations. Experiments on the modified IEEE 33-bus system demonstrate that reducing scheduling latency by one hour achieves over 30% emission reductions.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes a proactive ex-ante spatial-temporal carbon response framework to predict day-ahead nodal carbon intensity (NCI) and eliminate ex-post calculation latency.
- Develops a deep learning hierarchical architecture enhanced by a dual-stage attention mechanism and an LLM-based multi-agent cooperation system to handle renewable energy uncertainty.
- Introduces a spatial-temporal carbon scheduling model integrating geographically dispatchable loads (GDLs), including mobile energy storage systems (MESSs) and distributed data centers (DDCs).
- Demonstrates over 30% emission reduction under a one-hour reduction in carbon scheduling latency on the modified IEEE 33-bus system.

## Limitations

Evaluated specifically on a modified IEEE 33-bus system; broader scaling across larger transmission grids remains for future work.
