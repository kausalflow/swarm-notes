# Highly bipolar planetary nebula Hen 2-57 and its possible symbiotic core

**Authors**: Jaroslav Merc, J Mikołajewska, K Iłkiewicz, A Udalski
**Date**: 2026-09-17
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2609.19874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19874)

## Summary

This paper presents a comprehensive multi-wavelength analysis of the highly bipolar object Hen 2-57 using optical spectroscopy from the Southern African Large Telescope and long-term photometry from OGLE and NEOWISE. The object exhibits a distinct hourglass morphology, strong mid-infrared excess indicative of circumstellar dust, and complex kinematics where the central core differs from the extended lobes. Emission-line diagnostics reveal that while the lobes resemble a planetary nebula, the central core aligns with symbiotic systems. These findings support the conclusion that Hen 2-57 hosts a dust-enshrouded D-type symbiotic central engine shaping its nebula, serving as a close analogue to M 2-9.

## Key Contributions

- Presented a multi-wavelength analysis of the bipolar planetary nebula Hen 2-57 using optical spectroscopy from SALT and long-term photometry from OGLE and NEOWISE.
- Identified a pronounced hourglass morphology with an average lobe opening angle of ~53 degrees and significant north-south brightness asymmetry.
- Discovered that emission-line diagnostics of the central core are consistent with symbiotic stars rather than standard planetary nebulae.
- Demonstrated that Hen 2-57 likely hosts a dust-enshrouded D-type symbiotic central engine, acting as a close analogue to M 2-9.
