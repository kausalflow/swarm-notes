# Using analogues to investigate error growth and predictability in turbulence

**Authors**: Carlos Granero-Belinchón, Mickaël Bourgoin, Bérengère Dubrulle
**Date**: 2026-10-08
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.11796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11796)

## Summary

This paper investigates error growth and predictability in fully developed turbulence by applying Lorenz's analogue method to experimental turbulent velocity time series. The authors identify three distinct error growth regimes—chaotic exponential, algebraic spontaneous stochasticity, and saturation—and show that their exponents depend on the initial error due to turbulent intermittency. These findings validate Lorenz's classical intuitions while offering quantitative insights into turbulent predictability and extreme event forecasting.

## Key Contributions

- Revisits Lorenz's concept of analogues using experimental 1D turbulent velocity time series to investigate error growth and predictability.
- Successfully identifies three distinct error growth regimes: chaotic exponential, algebraic spontaneous stochasticity, and saturation.
- Reveals that Lyapunov exponents and algebraic growth exponents depend on the initial error, driven by turbulent intermittency and rare event sampling bias.
