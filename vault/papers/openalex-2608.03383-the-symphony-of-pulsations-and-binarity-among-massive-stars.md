# The symphony of pulsations and binarity among massive stars using HERMES spectroscopy and TESS photometry

**Authors**: K. Thomson-Paressant, D. M. Bowman, F. Nardini, L. J. A. Scott, J. Bodensteiner, T. Shenar, L. Mahy, G. Handler, N. Shitrit, I. Arcavi, M. Abdul-Masih, S. Simon-Diaz, P. Van Daele, A. J. Kalita, L. Dennis, J. Henneco, A. Tkachenko, H. Sana, H. Van Winckel
**Date**: 2026-08-04
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.03383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03383)

## Summary

This paper presents a large-scale homogeneous variability survey of 873 O- and B-type massive stars in the Northern hemisphere by combining high-resolution HERMES spectroscopy and 2-minute cadence TESS photometry. The authors utilise machine-learning spectral normalisation and frequency analysis to detect and characterise stellar pulsations, binarity, and rotational modulation. Results show that over 93 percent of the sample exhibits photometric variability, with pulsations identified in 82 percent and binarity in at least 14 percent of the targets, providing a comprehensive statistical framework for future asteroseismic modelling.

## Key Contributions

- Conducted one of the largest homogeneous variability surveys of 873 O- and B-type massive stars using high-resolution HERMES spectroscopy and TESS photometry.
- Identified photometric variability in over 93 percent of the sample, with pulsations present in 82 percent dominated by beta Cep and slowly pulsating B-type stars.
- Established a statistical framework combining radial velocity measurements and frequency analysis to study the interplay of binarity, pulsation, and rotation in massive stars.
