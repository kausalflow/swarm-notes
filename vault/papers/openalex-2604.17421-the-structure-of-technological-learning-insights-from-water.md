# The structure of technological learning: Insights from water electrolysis for cost forecasting, policy, and strategy

**Authors**: Mohamed Atouife, Jesse Jenkins
**Date**: 2026-10-27
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2604.17421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17421)

## Summary

Forecasting the cost evolution of emerging clean technologies like water electrolysis is hindered by parametric and structural uncertainties in traditional learning curves. Using water electrolysis as a case study, this paper evaluates how contrasting learning structures—such as shared versus fragmented learning across variants and regions—produce drastically different cost paths. The findings highlight the sensitivity of technology strategy and policy design to these modeling choices, motivating the routine application of multi-framework evaluations.

## Key Contributions

- Evaluates how different learning structures (shared vs fragmented learning) alter expected cost paths using water electrolysis as a case study.
- Demonstrates that plausible modeling choices for learning curves generate widely divergent cost trajectories and implications for policy.
- Proposes routinely applying multiple learning frameworks to stress-test technology scale-up planning and energy-systems modeling.

## Limitations

Focuses specifically on water electrolysis as a case study without exhaustive cross-technology validation in the abstract.
