# Statistical Noise and Missing Forcing Limit Estimates of Earth's Feedback from Prescribed Sea-Surface Temperature Simulations

**Authors**: Gergana Gyuleva, Reto Knutti, Robin Noyelle, Urs Beyerle, Erich Fischer, Sebastian Sippel
**Date**: 2026-08-13
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.13219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13219)

## Summary

Earth's feedback parameter is frequently estimated by prescribing observed sea-surface temperatures (SSTs) to atmospheric models using fixed pre-industrial forcing and moving-window regressions. This paper tests these assumptions using controlled fully-coupled historical and control simulations, demonstrating that such prescribed-SST experiments fail to capture the true coupled feedback evolution due to missing time-varying atmospheric forcing and inherent statistical noise. Consequently, feedback trends detected on timescales under ~100 years cannot be reliably attributed to evolving SST patterns.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrated that prescribed-SST atmospheric model experiments fail to accurately capture the coupled feedback evolution of the climate system.
- Identified missing prescribed atmospheric forcing and statistical noise from moving-window regressions as the primary causes of failure in standard feedback estimation methods.
- Proved that trends in the climate feedback parameter detected on timescales shorter than ~100 years are indistinguishable from statistical noise.
