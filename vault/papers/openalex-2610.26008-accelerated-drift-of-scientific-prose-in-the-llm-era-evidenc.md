# Accelerated Drift of Scientific Prose in the LLM Era: Evidence from an Interrupted Time-Series Analysis

**Authors**: Hanna Hubarava, Benjamin Victor Ineichen
**Date**: 2026-10-19
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.26008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26008)

## Summary

This paper investigates how the widespread adoption of LLM-based writing tools has altered scientific prose by performing an interrupted time-series regression analysis on preprints from arXiv and medRxiv around the November 2022 breakpoint. The authors examine multiple feature families—including vocabulary, punctuation, readability, and syntax—and discover substantial post-2022 changes such as increased word length, reduced readability, and decreased dependency-tree depth. Furthermore, the findings reveal that individual linguistic markers follow diverse trajectories, highlighting the need to monitor scientific writing as a multidimensional, evolving system rather than through isolated markers.

## Key Contributions

- Investigates the impact of conversational LLMs on scientific writing norms using an interrupted time-series regression analysis around the November 2022 ChatGPT breakpoint.
- Analyzes pre- and post-2022 trajectories across vocabulary, punctuation, readability, and syntax using preprint abstracts from arXiv (AI, statistics, quantitative biology) and medRxiv.
- Identifies substantial post-2022 shifts including increased average word length, reduced readability, and declines in dependency-tree depth and sentence-level depth variation.
- Demonstrates that individual lexical markers exhibit heterogeneous dynamics, with some persistently accelerating and others rising before receding.
