# Toward a Complete Interpretation of Global 21 cm Absorption Signals Using the Radio CMB

**Authors**: Hannah Fronenberg
**Date**: 2026-10-29
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.40300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40300)

## Summary

The paper addresses the degeneracy in global 21 cm absorption signals (such as the EDGES anomaly) between cold gas and excess radio background interpretations. By proposing to combine global signal measurements with resonant 21 cm absorption imprints on CMB anisotropies, the approach isolates the radio background temperature without requiring astrophysical modeling. Through forecasting four interferometric configurations, the study shows that a compact array can detect an EDGES-like optical depth at a statistically significant level, offering a model-independent interpretive framework for cosmic dawn.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes combining CMB hydrogen optical depth measurements with the global 21 cm signal to break the degeneracy between cold gas and excess radio background interpretations.
- Forecasts four interferometric configurations, demonstrating that a compact 55 x 55 array of 6 m dishes observing for 4000 hours can establish an EDGES-like optical depth at 4.2 sigma.
- Provides a model-independent route to interpreting global 21 cm detections without relying on complex astrophysical modeling.

## Limitations

Fiducial cosmic dawn histories require roughly an order of magnitude more collecting area than the compact array configuration.
