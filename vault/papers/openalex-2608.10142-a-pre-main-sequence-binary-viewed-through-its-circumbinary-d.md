# A Pre-main-sequence Binary Viewed Through Its Circumbinary Disk: A New Phase for KH 15D

**Authors**: Catrina M. Hamilton, Adi B. Chacko, G. R. Carson, Aidan J. Pidgeon, C. N. Foley, Hillel Finder, Kyle Gimmy, Aleezah Ali, W. Herbst, D. Froebrich, B. A. Skiff, Bart Staels, Shawn Dvorak, J. Eislöffel, Teryn Huff, Gavin Frueh, Abigail Mead, Lia M. Gilmore, Robert Boyle
**Date**: 2026-08-31
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.10142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10142)

## Summary

The paper presents an analysis of ground-based time-series photometry for the pre-main-sequence binary system KH 15D, incorporating new data from October 2022 to April 2025 alongside historical measurements spanning 75 years. The findings show that the historical era of large-amplitude photometric variability has ended, establishing a revised photometric period of 48.365 days. By evaluating optical depths and out-of-eclipse colors, the authors provide evidence that the trailing edge of the circumbinary disk is more transparent than the leading edge, offering a new observational window into the planet-forming zone.

## Key Contributions

- Analyzed a complete set of ground-based time-series photometry for the pre-main-sequence binary system KH 15D, incorporating newly presented 2022–2025 data.
- Demonstrated that the era of large-amplitude photometric variability has ended, establishing a revised photometric period of P = 48.365 days phasing data over 75 years.
- Determined the effective optical depth from 2002 to 2025 under the assumption of gray extinction and showed that current out-of-eclipse colors reflect light from both binary stars.
- Provided observational evidence that the trailing edge of the circumbinary disk is more transparent than the leading edge and highlighted opportunities for studying the planet-forming zone.
