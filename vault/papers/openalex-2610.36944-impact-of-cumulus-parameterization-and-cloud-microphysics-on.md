# Impact of Cumulus Parameterization and Cloud Microphysics on WRF Simulation of an Extreme Rainfall Event over Maharashtra, India: A 3 by 3 Factorial Assessment

**Authors**: Devesh, Satyam Kumar, Akshay Sunil
**Date**: 2026-10-27
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.36944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36944)

## Summary

This study evaluates the sensitivity of Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model simulations of an extreme monsoon rainfall event over Maharashtra, India, using a 3 x 3 factorial design of cumulus and cloud microphysics schemes. A descriptive factorial decomposition reveals that cumulus parameterization dominates the variance in model performance (explaining over 84-97% of variation in mean rainfall, RMSE, and spatial correlation), whereas cloud microphysics plays a secondary role. Specific scheme combinations reveal distinct regional biases, highlighting challenges in grey-zone resolution and precipitation placement.

## Key Contributions

- Evaluated a 3 x 3 factorial design of Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) simulations combining three microphysics schemes (WSM6, WDM6, Thompson) and three cumulus schemes (Kain-Fritsch, Tiedtke, Grell-Freitas) for extreme monsoon rainfall over Maharashtra, India.
- Demonstrated via factorial decomposition that cumulus parameterization accounted for 97.3% of across-experiment variation in mean rainfall, 88.2% in RMSE, and 84.7% in spatial correlation, while cloud microphysics contributed only 2.0%, 2.1%, and 9.3%, respectively.
- Identified that Tiedtke achieved the smallest mean biases and RMSE while Kain-Fritsch produced higher spatial correlations, and that all configurations displaced the rainfall maximum approximately 140-195 km northward.

## Limitations

Single-event, single-member experimental design does not establish a universally optimal physics configuration.
