# Non-stationary time series attribution for heatwaves over Europe

**Authors**: Pascal Meurer, Sebastian Buschow, Svenja Szemkus, Petra Friederichs
**Date**: 2026-10-07
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2601.05841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05841)

## Summary

This study introduces a non-stationary time series attribution approach using bivariate extreme value theory within a Markov process framework to evaluate extreme heat events over Europe. By comparing observational data from ERA5 against CMIP6 historical natural-only and anthropogenic forcing simulations condensed via the extremal pattern index, the method evaluates the likelihood of temporal dependence during heatwaves. The findings reveal very strong evidence for anthropogenic drivers since the 21st century—and as early as the 1960s for central and southern Europe—while noting that changes are primarily driven by mean warming shifts rather than alterations in temporal dependence or extreme value distribution shape.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes a non-stationary Markov process approach utilizing bivariate extreme value theory to attribute complete time series during extreme events to anthropogenic forcing.
- Employs the extremal pattern index (EPI) to condense spatial fields for compact description of spatial extremes in European heatwave attribution.
- Demonstrates via CMIP6 and ERA5 comparisons that anthropogenic influence on European heatwaves could be proven in the 1960s for central and southern Europe, with no reliable signal beyond a general mean temperature shift regarding temporal dependence or extreme value shape.
