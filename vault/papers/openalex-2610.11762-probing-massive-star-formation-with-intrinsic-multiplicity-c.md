# Probing massive star formation with intrinsic multiplicity constraints from TESS eclipsing binaries

**Authors**: Jasmine Vrancken, Alex Kemp, Andrew Tkachenko, Conny Aerts, Hugues Sana
**Date**: 2026-10-28
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.11762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11762)

## Summary

This paper investigates massive star formation mechanisms by analyzing an all-sky sample of eclipsing binaries using TESS photometric time series, Gaia DR3 distances, and 2MASS photometry. By rigorously correcting for geometric and observational selection effects, the authors derive bias-corrected binary fractions and orbital parameter distributions for primary masses ranging from 1.1 M_⊙ to 47 M_⊙. The results reveal a gradual transition in binary properties across stellar masses with agreement for lower-mass systems under disk fragmentation and migration, providing empirical constraints for stellar evolution theories.

## Key Contributions

- Derived bias-corrected binary fractions and orbital parameter distributions for an all-sky sample of eclipsing binaries spanning primary masses from 1.1 M_⊙ to 47 M_⊙ using TESS photometric time series, Gaia DR3 distances, and 2MASS near-infrared photometry.
- Found that for primary masses up to ~5 M_⊙, observed orbital parameter distributions agree with disk fragmentation followed by migration.
- Observed a gradual continuity in binary properties across F- to O-type stars without requiring a sharp transition in binary properties.

## Limitations

At higher stellar masses, an apparent deficit of very short period systems was observed, which may be affected by observational incompleteness; alternative formation channels may also contribute alongside disk fragmentation.
