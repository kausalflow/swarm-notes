# LLM-based Agents for Forecasting and Prediction: Methods, Training, Evaluation, and Applications

**Authors**: Xiaogang Xu, Jiaqi Tang, Jianmin Chen, Yingying Yan, Zhenchao Tang, Xiangxin Zhou, Xiaobin Hu, Wei Wei, WU Jin-feng, Qifeng Chen, Lu Zhou, Jiafei Wu, Zhe Liu, Jianwei Yin, Weimin Zheng
**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2608.23058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23058)

## Summary

This paper provides a comprehensive survey of large language model (LLM) based agents for forecasting and prediction, categorizing architectures into standalone workflows, tool- and retrieval-augmented systems, and hybrid models. The authors review training methods, evaluation protocols, and common failure modes including input sensitivity, negative ablation results, and benchmark contamination. Furthermore, they outline applications across diverse domains such as finance, weather, health, energy, and operations while discussing critical future research directions.

## Key Contributions

- Organizes LLM-based forecasting agent architectures into three distinct categories: standalone workflows, tool- and retrieval-augmented agents, and hybrid systems pairing LLMs with statistical or foundation models.
- Reviews training methodologies, evaluation protocols, and empirical challenges such as input sensitivity, ablation failures, and potential benchmark contamination.
- Surveys downstream forecasting applications across finance, weather, health, energy, and operations while highlighting key evaluation benchmarks.
- Identifies critical open challenges including distribution shift calibration, contamination-resistant evaluation, cost-accuracy trade-offs, and deployed feedback loops.

## Limitations

The survey highlights prominent limitations in existing LLM-based forecasting literature, notably measurement constraints, high input sensitivity, frequent lack of accuracy gains over baselines, and severe risks of data contamination.
